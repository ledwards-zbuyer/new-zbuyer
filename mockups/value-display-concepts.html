Value display concepts — five ways

The brief: show the estimates without handing the seller one precise number to anchor on — protecting agent members from leads with fixed expectations built on an errant automated valuation. Every concept below is variance-forward and repeats the same ground truth: a real number only exists after verification and an inspection. 1 · Brackets, not numbers

The strongest anchor-avoidance: no digits at all. The lead learns the neighborhood of value ("the $300Ks") and how the three estimate types stack, but the first precise number they ever hear comes from the member — the report sets the stage instead of stealing the scene.

2 · Value forecast

The weather framing: nobody argues with a forecast the way they argue with a price tag. High / low pairs read as naturally provisional, each source carries its own confidence meter, and "conditions" (market, condition, timing) explain why the number moves until it's verified.

3 · The path to a real number

Process over price: the hero isn't a value, it's the journey. Automated estimates start wide, each verification step visibly narrows the cone, and the final "your verified offer" slot is deliberately empty — the report literally cannot show a real number yet, and says so with its shape.

4 · Your home among the market

Zoom out to the distribution: instead of pricing THIS house, show the market it lives in — recent nearby sales as anonymous dots, with a soft band where homes like yours landed. Value reads as a spread that condition and timing decide, which is exactly the conversation a member wants to have.

Each dot is a nearby sale in the last 12 months. The shaded band is where homes like yours landed — where yours lands depends on condition, timing, and the way you sell.

Your spot in the band gets verified in person — no automated number can place it exactly.

5 · Spread-first dials

Make uncertainty the headline stat: each source's big number is its SPREAD — how much wiggle it carries — not its value. The endpoints appear only in small type. A lead reads "this tool carries ±$9–12K of wiggle" before any price, which frames every figure as provisional by construction.
